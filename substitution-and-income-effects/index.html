Hicksian decomposition of price changes

A price change in good X alters both the relative price ratio between goods and the real purchasing power of nominal income. John Hicks' method separates these two forces by holding consumer utility constant.

Central decomposition identity

\Delta X_C = \Delta X_S + \Delta X_D

Moving from the initial equilibrium bundle A to the final bundle C passes through an intermediate compensated bundle B located on the initial indifference curve U_1.

Substitution effect
\Delta X_S = X_B - X_A

Movement along the initial indifference curve U_1 caused purely by the new relative price ratio P_{X2} / P_Y.

Income effect
\Delta X_D = X_C - X_B

Parallel shift from the compensated budget line BL_H to the final budget line BL_2 as nominal income M is restored.

Total price effect
\Delta X_C = X_C - X_A

Net change in observed quantity demanded between initial equilibrium A on BL_1 and final equilibrium C on BL_2.

Three canonical cases for a price decrease (P_{X2} < P_{X1})

Direction of substitution, income, and total effects depending on the nature of good X.

Normal good

\Delta X_C > 0

Higher real purchasing power increases consumption of good X. Both substitution and income effects work in the same direction.

\Delta X_S > 0, \quad \Delta X_D > 0

Inferior good

\Delta X_C > 0

Higher real income reduces demand for good X, so the income effect is negative, yet weaker than the positive substitution effect.

\Delta X_S > 0, \quad \Delta X_D < 0, \quad |\Delta X_D| < \Delta X_S

Giffen good*

\Delta X_C < 0

A theoretical boundary case where a strong negative income effect outweighs the positive substitution effect, reducing total demand when price falls.

\Delta X_S > 0, \quad \Delta X_D < 0, \quad |\Delta X_D| > \Delta X_S
*Methodological note on Giffen goods: Giffen goods are primarily theoretical constructs in microeconomics. Empirical literature lacks unambiguous evidence of persistent Giffen behavior in real markets; the concept defines the theoretical boundary of the law of demand.

Workshop tasks

Task 1: Normal good and 50% price reduction

Foundational

Select a normal good with nominal income M = 120, P_{X1} = 6.0, P_{X2} = 3.0, and P_Y = 4.0. Identify the coordinates of bundles A, B, and C. What share of the total increase in good X comes from the substitution effect versus the income effect?

Initial equilibrium: A = (10.00; 15.00). After P_X drops to 3.0, the Hicksian compensated bundle is B = (14.14; 10.61), and the final equilibrium is C = (20.00; 15.00). The total effect equals \Delta X_C = +10.00 units, consisting of \Delta X_S = +4.14 (41.4%) from substitution and \Delta X_D = +5.86 (58.6%) from higher real income.

Task 2: Inferior good and opposing effects

Intermediate

Switch the good type to inferior with M = 120, P_{X1} = 8.0, P_{X2} = 4.0, and P_Y = 4.0. Explain why bundle C lies to the left of bundle B even though final consumption X_C exceeds initial consumption X_A.

Lower P_X generates a positive substitution effect (X_B > X_A). Simultaneously, higher real purchasing power induces the consumer to shift away from the inferior good toward good Y (X_C < X_B). Because the substitution effect outweighs the negative income effect (|\Delta X_D| < \Delta X_S), net quantity demanded rises (X_C > X_A).

Task 3: Hypothetical Giffen good and demand reversal

Theoretical analysis

Load the Giffen good case (M = 100, P_{X1} = 6.0, P_{X2} = 3.0, P_Y = 4.0). Compare the horizontal positions of A, B, and C. Which structural conditions would be required for this theoretical paradox to emerge?

In the hypothetical Giffen case, bundle C lies to the left of initial bundle A (X_C < X_A). The negative income effect exceeds the positive substitution effect in absolute magnitude (|\Delta X_D| > \Delta X_S). Theoretically, this requires a strongly inferior staple good that absorbs a large share of the household budget and lacks close substitutes.

Notation reference

BL_1, BL_2

Initial and final budget lines for nominal income M.

BL_H, M_H

Hicksian compensated budget line and adjusted income keeping utility at U_1.

\Delta X_S, \Delta X_D

Substitution effect (X_B - X_A) and income effect (X_C - X_B).

U_1, U_2

Initial and final indifference curves representing consumer utility levels.

Constant utility approach

Hicksian compensation (BL_H)

Hicks adjusts nominal income to M_H so the consumer can reach the exact same indifference curve U_1 under the new price ratio P_{X2} / P_Y. The compensated line BL_H is tangent to U_1 at point B.

Advantage: Directly links to compensated (Hicksian) demand curves and expenditure minimization.
Constant purchasing power approach

Slutsky compensation (comparison)

Unlike Hicks, Eugen Slutsky pivots the budget line through the original bundle A so the consumer can afford the exact same basket at new prices. This places the intermediate bundle on a slightly higher indifference curve than U_1.

Advantage: Observable directly from market prices and initial quantities without knowing utility functions.
