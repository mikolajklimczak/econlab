# **1\. Getting started with the application**

EconLab's Market Mechanism is an interactive web tool designed to help you practice supply, demand, and market equilibrium analysis at your own pace. It runs directly in your browser on a laptop, tablet, or smartphone without requiring registration or software downloads.

At the top right of the screen, you can switch the interface language between English and Polish at any time. Using the toggle is a convenient way to check economic terms in both languages while keeping the exact same equations, graphs, and exercises on screen.

# **2\. Reviewing core equilibrium concepts**

Before adjusting sliders or solving equations, read through the opening theory section ("Market equilibrium theory and price shocks"). It summarizes the core rules you need for both workshops and exams:

* **Equilibrium condition:** A market clears at the equilibrium price (PE) where consumer quantity demanded equals producer quantity supplied (QD \= QS \= QE).  
* **Disequilibrium adjustment:** When the market price is below PE, quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied (QD \> QS), creating a shortage that pushes prices upward. When the price is above PE, quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded (QS \> QD), creating a surplus that pushes prices downward.  
* **Non-price determinants:** Changes in income, prices of related goods, tastes, demographics, expectations, input costs, technology, taxes, subsidies, number of sellers, or external shocks shift an entire curve rather than moving a point along it.  
* **Four canonical shifts:** Review the summary cards for single shifts in demand or supply under ceteris paribus so you can predict immediately whether PE and QE rise or fall.

As you read each non-price shifter, pause and think of one concrete product or service affected by that factor before moving to the simulator.

# **3\. Experimenting with the interactive simulator**

The middle section of the page contains an interactive market simulator starting from a baseline equilibrium of E0 \= (PE \= 50.0, QE \= 50.0), defined by the linear equations QD \= 85 \- 1.7·P and QS \= 10 \+ 1.4·P. Explore its controls in three stages:

1. **Test prices away from equilibrium:** Keep the demand and supply curves at their baseline positions and move the Market price (P) slider above and below 50\. Watch the horizontal gap between the two curves on the graph and read the Market adjustment mechanism panel to see how the exact size of the shortage or surplus changes with price.  
2. **Modify curve slopes and intercepts:** Use the Market equation parameters sliders (a, b, c, d) to make demand or supply steeper, flatter, or larger. Observe how changing a slope rotates the curve around its intercept, whereas changing an intercept shifts the curve horizontally.  
3. **Apply parallel curve shifts and quick scenarios:** Move the Demand shift (ΔD) and Supply shift (ΔS) sliders – or click one of the Quick scenarios buttons – to see how the market moves from initial equilibrium E0 to a new equilibrium E1. Check the Step-by-step equilibrium calculation panel below the graph to trace how the equation QD \= QS is solved algebraically for your exact parameter choices.

# **4\. Working through the analytical tasks**

Below the simulator, the "Market problem-solving tasks" section presents applied scenarios – such as simultaneous demand and supply shocks in an artisan bakery market – that mirror exam and workshop problems.

To get the most benefit from these tasks, follow a three-step routine:

1. **Predict the direction on paper first:** Before looking at the solution, identify which curve or curves shift, sketch a quick graph in your notebook, and determine whether PE and QE increase, decrease, or become ambiguous.  
2. **Solve the algebra by hand:** Set the new demand equation equal to the new supply equation (QD \= QS), solve for PE, and substitute PE back into either equation to find QE.  
3. **Verify in the application:** Check your final numbers and reasoning against the task feedback, or reproduce the exact equations in the simulator above to inspect the visual shift from E0 to E1.

# **5\. Testing understanding with the self-assessment quiz**

Once you have practiced with the simulator and analytical tasks, complete the randomized multiple-choice quiz. The quiz draws from a pool of twelve questions covering equilibrium definitions, shortages and surpluses, single curve shifts, and simultaneous shocks.

Treat each attempt as a diagnostic check rather than a one-time test. Whenever you miss a question, note the concept involved, return to the simulator to recreate that exact scenario, and observe how the curves and algebraic steps behave before retaking the quiz.

# **6\. Self-study checklist and practice prompts**

Before your next workshop or exam, confirm that you can complete each of the following exercises independently:

* **Disequilibrium check:** Set P \= 35 in the baseline simulator, calculate the exact shortage (QD \- QS) by hand, and verify your number on the graph.  
* **Single supply shock:** Apply a negative supply shift (ΔS \< 0\) while holding demand constant, and explain in two sentences why equilibrium price rises while equilibrium quantity falls.  
* **Simultaneous opposite shocks:** Set ΔD \= \+20 and ΔS \= \-20 in the simulator, solve for the new PE and QE algebraically, and explain what happens to PE if both shifts have the same magnitude but opposite signs.  
* **Ambiguous outcome test:** Experiment with two simultaneous rightward shifts (ΔD \> 0 and ΔS \> 0\) of different sizes to see why QE always increases while PE can rise, fall, or stay unchanged.  
* **Terminology check:** Switch the interface between English and Polish and verify that you can explain equilibrium, shortage, surplus, and curve shifts accurately in your course language.
